🟢 General Waste: Office paper, kitchen waste, packaging boxes. Non-infectious municipal solid waste.

Public Systems Architecture

Urban Waste Management Crisis & SBM 2.0 Solution

A Software-Only, Zero-IoT, Multi-Stakeholder Civic Governance Engine

1. Project Overview & Architecture

This platform is a software-only, multi-role municipal civic utility designed to eliminate the prohibitive capital costs and maintenance failure rates of hardware IoT sensors. By combining geo-referenced crowdsourcing, verified source segregation, institution self-audits, route optimization, and statutory biomedical ledger tracking, it equips Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) with end-to-end accountability from citizen generator to processing facility.

2. Root-Cause Breakdown

  • Low Source Segregation: Mixed municipal solid waste contaminates recyclables and prevents biological composting and biomethanation.
  • Weak Municipal Capacity: Sub-optimal field crew allocations and lack of algorithmic routing lead to uncollected overflow points.
  • Funding-to-Ground Gaps: Capital grants often stall before reaching ward-level sanitation infrastructure and sanitary worker safety gear.
  • Treatment Capacity Lag: Waste generation exponentially outpaces centralized processing facilities and waste-to-energy plants.
  • Overloaded Landfills: Legacy dump sites exceed saturation limits, creating toxic leachate and methane emission hazards.
  • Informal Sector Left Unregulated: Informal waste pickers (kabadiwalas) handle over 60% of urban recycling without institutional formalization or social security.

3. Indian Waste Governance Timeline (2000 - 2026)

MSW Rules 2000
First national statutory framework defining urban local body responsibilities for municipal solid waste management.
SBM-Urban 2014
Flagship Swachh Bharat Mission launched by the Government of India to achieve 100% open-defecation-free status and scientifically manage urban solid waste.
SWM Rules 2016
Replaced MSW 2000; legally mandated source segregation into Wet, Dry, and Domestic Hazardous waste streams for all waste generators and bulk facilities.
SBM 2.0 Mandates (2024-2026)
Transitioned mandate toward "Garbage-Free Cities" (GFC), biomining and bio-remediation of 100% legacy dumpsites, and circular economy integration.
80.26% Processing Rate Milestone (2024)
National statutory processing capacity reached landmark 80.26% milestone across Indian urban local bodies, up from under 18% in 2014.
SWM Rules 2026 (Mandatory Source Segregation)
Enforces mandatory nationwide source segregation with automated compliance auditing, bulk generator digital self-certifications, and integration of informal recycling markets.
